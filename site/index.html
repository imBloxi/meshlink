Messages that don't need the internet.

MeshLink sends end-to-end encrypted messages through a mesh of $3 ESP8266 radio boards. No router, no cloud, no sign-up, and nothing for a server to log.

Three layers, each with its own job

The app protects the message. The boards protect the mesh. The login protects the boards.

App to app

Your PC encrypts and signs every message before it leaves. Direct messages use X25519 and XSalsa20-Poly1305. Rooms add Ed25519 signatures and rotate their key when someone is removed.

Board to board

Boards flood frames over ESP-NOW radio, drop duplicates and rate-limit. Each frame carries a keyed BLAKE2s tag, so outsiders can't inject traffic.

App to board

USB for the admin, or the board's own hotspot for guests. Guests sign in with a per-network pseudonym that can't be linked to your account.

The full wire format is in PROTOCOL.md.

Be chatting in ten minutes

You need two boards (a D1 mini or NodeMCU, about $3 each) and two PCs. No boards yet? The app has a simulator.

  1. Install the appDownload the Windows installer from Releases and check it against SHA256SUMS.txt. It is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn you. Choose More info, then Run anyway.
  2. Flash two boardsPlug in a board and choose Connect, then Flash a new board. The app checks the firmware's SHA-256 before writing it.
  3. Chat, then verifyConnect each board to a PC. Contacts appear on their own. Before you trust one, meet in person and compare the safety number under Contact, then Verify.

What MeshLink does not hide

A privacy tool should say this plainly.

  • That you use it, and when. Radio is visible. A listener sees timing and volume, and which board sent first.
  • Your announces. Your name and public keys are broadcast so people can find you. Stealth mode stops automatic announces.
  • Forward secrecy. Keys are static. A key stolen later opens traffic recorded earlier.
  • A compromised PC. Malware running as you can use your keys.
  • Physical access to a board. It stores its network key and Wi-Fi passwords in flash, unencrypted.
  • Jamming. Nobody can stop radio jamming.
